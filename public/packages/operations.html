What it looks like in real life

One example, start to finish.

Example: the estimate follow-up chain

  1. You send an estimate and note it in the workflow — the follow-up clock starts.
  2. Two days later the agent checks in politely, answers basic questions from your approved information, and offers next steps.
  3. At day five, one more follow-up. If the customer is ready, the agent books the call or job and sends confirmation and reminders.
  4. Anything about price changes or discounts stops at your approval point and lands in your lap, organized — never improvised.

What Operations actually means

No jargon — what you're really buying with this package.

Operations means this: most businesses don't lose customers inside one task — they lose them between tasks. The inquiry that never got booked. The estimate nobody followed up. Operations covers your core workflow plus the handoffs connected to it, as one chain with one set of rules: question → booking → reminder → follow-up, with your approval points marked at every step.

Questions people ask about Operations

The things owners usually want to know before this package makes sense.

What counts as a 'handoff'?

Any moment work passes from one step to the next: an inquiry becoming a booking, a booking needing a reminder, an estimate needing a follow-up. Each handoff is a place things usually get dropped — Operations is built to cover exactly those places.

Who writes the follow-ups?

You approve the wording and the schedule up front — for example, a polite check-in two days after an estimate and one more at five days. The agent sends within those approved words; it never invents prices, discounts, or promises to close a sale.

What happens when a customer replies with something unexpected?

The chain handles the expected. Anything off-script — a complaint, a negotiation, a weird request — is routed to you, organized, with the conversation attached. You're never left wondering what the agent said.

How is this different from Foundation?

Foundation covers one workflow by itself. Operations covers that workflow plus the steps that touch it, so the whole chain holds together instead of one strong link and three weak ones.
