What this can look like

Four everyday workflows, the way they'd actually run. Yours would be mapped to your business, your tools, and your approval points.

A new customer inquiry

  1. A customer asks about hours, services, or booking
  2. The agent answers from your approved information
  3. It collects name, contact, and what they need
  4. Anything about price waits for you

An estimate follow-up

  1. You send an estimate; the clock starts
  2. The agent follows up on the schedule you approved
  3. Basic questions get answered; discounts wait for you
  4. Ready customers get booked, not lost

Appointment booking

  1. The agent offers the times you make available
  2. The customer picks; confirmation goes out
  3. A reminder follows before the appointment
  4. Changes and exceptions land with you, organized

Routine paperwork intake

  1. The agent asks for the same information, every time
  2. Answers arrive complete and in one place
  3. Missing pieces get one polite chase
  4. You review a prepared summary, not a messy inbox

How it works

Three steps, in the open, before anything is automated.

1 — Identify a useful task

We map a repeatable business task as it happens today and decide whether an agent could help.

2 — Document human review

Together we mark the points where a person must check, approve, or stop the work before it continues.

3 — Hand off the agreed next steps

You receive a written handoff covering the agreed work, tool-connection details, approval points, setup notes, and what happens next.

Frequently asked questions

Plain answers, no jargon. Anything else — ask in the form at the top and a person will reply.

Do I need to know how AI works?

No. You describe the work in plain language — the same questions, follow-ups, or paperwork that eat your week. Working out the technical side is our job, not yours.

What happens after I send the form?

A person reviews every request. If there's a sensible next step, you'll get a follow-up at the email you gave, with the agreed scope and approval points in writing before anything is set up.

Will an agent send things to my customers without my say-so?

Only within the boundaries you approve first. Prices, refunds, and anything else you name always wait for your approval — those points are written into the plan before work starts, not added later.

How much does it cost?

Packages are $1,200, $2,400, or $4,800 per month depending on scope. No package guarantees a result — the first decision is whether the work is a sensible fit at all, and there's no charge for asking.

What kind of work fits best?

Work that repeats: answering the same customer questions, booking and confirming appointments, following up on estimates and leads, and gathering routine paperwork. If it happens every week in roughly the same way, it's worth a conversation.

Glossary — the words on this page, in plain English

If a word here ever sounds like jargon, this is what we actually mean by it.

Managed agent

An AI helper set up and looked after for you, to do a defined piece of your business's repeating work. "Managed" means a person scopes it, watches it, and stays responsible for it.

Workflow

One job that repeats, from start to finish — for example, "a new inquiry comes in, gets answered, gets booked, gets a reminder." We always start with just one.

Approval point

A step where the work stops and waits for your yes before continuing — like sending a price, a refund, or a message you're not comfortable automating.

Scope

Exactly what the agent will and won't do, written down. If it isn't in the scope, it doesn't happen.

Handoff

The written summary you receive: the agreed work, the approval points, the setup notes, and what happens next — so nothing lives only in someone's head.

Human review

A person checking the work at the points that matter, instead of software running on its own. Every inquiry sent from this site gets it.
